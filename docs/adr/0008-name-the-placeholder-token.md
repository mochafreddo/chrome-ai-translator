# Call it a Placeholder Token, homonym and all

Status: accepted

The thing that stands in for a link, emphasis, or code span while a translation is in flight had no name in `CONTEXT.md`, and five vocabularies for it coexisted. The code's identifiers say token — `token`, `openToken`, `closeToken`, `expectedTokens`, `getTokenLikeLiterals`, `createTokenNamespace` — in both `extension/inline-block.js` and `extension/full-page-markdown.js`. The failure codes say token: `markdown.token_missing` and its three siblings, `structure.token_*`, and `token_parent_changed` on Inline Translation's side. One prose line in `full-page-markdown.js` says protected span, and the atom kinds beside it say `protected-link`. The reader is shown neither: `sidepanel-failure.js` says "a link or code marker". And the documentation's own prose already says placeholder — ADR-0005 on "the placeholder tokens the chunk was sent", ADR-0006 on requiring "every placeholder back byte-for-byte", and the Translation Chunk entry on an answer "that comes back without the placeholders it was sent". The decision is that the term is **Placeholder Token**, and that the homonym it creates is accepted and fenced rather than avoided.

Naming it was deferred on 2026-08-14, out of issue #24's diff, because the obvious name collides. It collides worse than that: `token` in this project already carries two unrelated meanings, not one. A model token is what the reader is billed for — `maxOutputTokens`, `normalizeMaxOutputTokens`, "Translation output reached its token limit." A correlation token is what pairs an inline result with the request that asked for it — `createInlineRuntimeCorrelationToken`, `correlationToken`, `releaseTokens`. Adding a third sense to a word that already has two is the cost of this decision and the reason it needed one.

It is paid because the alternatives cost more, and they cost more in a specific way: they leave the failure codes stranded. **Protected Span** has the better concept boundary — it names the thing being protected rather than the stand-in — and it has more footing in the code than the one prose line suggests, in `protected-link` and in the "protected atom" comment. But #48 lifts both validators into one module while requiring that the failure codes each translation reports are unchanged, and those codes are `token_*`. Choosing Protected Span would rename every identifier and leave the codes reading in the retired vocabulary, taking five vocabularies down to two rather than to one, and it would make #48 a rename with a consolidation inside it instead of a consolidation. **Inline Marker** matches the reader-facing words and collides with nothing, and was rejected on a different ground: five glossary entries begin with `Inline` and every one of them belongs to Inline Translation. Putting that prefix on the one term both translations share would state the opposite of what the entry exists to say.

That sharing is itself worth stating. The glossary's preamble says most of its vocabulary exists to keep the two translations apart, and this is the first term they hold in common, so the preamble now names the exception and the entry explains it. The two translations share this term because they meet the same problem — a model that must be free to reorder words around a span it may not rewrite — and reach it with the same contract and the same four failures, not because they resemble each other. #48 gives them one implementation of it, which is the sharing made structural rather than coincidental.

The reader keeps hearing "a link or code marker". Aligning the reader's wording to the glossary would be the obvious tidying and would be wrong: a reader has no idea what a placeholder token is, and the four failures they can be shown are already the point at which the extension is asking for patience. The paraphrase is deliberate, it is one hop from the term, and it is not a sixth vocabulary.

## Consequences

- `Token` alone is not a term in this project and never becomes one. Every use is qualified — placeholder, model, or correlation — and the entry's _Avoid_ line names all three so that a reader who meets a bare `token` in code knows to ask which.
- #48 is a consolidation rather than a rename. The identifiers and the failure codes already spell the settled name, so lifting the enumeration and the nesting walk into one module changes no vocabulary; the `markdown.token_*` and `structure.token_*` codes are now the glossary-endorsed spelling rather than merely the incumbent one.
- The atom kinds stay internal. `ATOM`, `OPEN`, `CLOSE`, and `protected-link` are the entry kinds each translation's adapter maps onto the shared module in #48, and none of them is promoted to a glossary term by this decision. A Placeholder Token is what those kinds all produce.
- `sidepanel-failure.js` is not to be brought into line with the glossary. Its four sentences are the reader's vocabulary, and the entry says so, so a later pass reading them as drift has an answer already written down.
- The lowercase "placeholder" already in the Translation Chunk entry and in ADR-0005 and ADR-0006 now refers to this term. Those documents were written before it existed and are left as they stand; the term is capitalized where a document means it as a term.
- Nothing in `extension/` changed and no check covers this. `tests/static-assets.test.js` does not read `CONTEXT.md`, and `npm test` is unaffected either way — it was run for this change and is green.

## Do not revert this

Renaming to Protected Span later will look like a correction rather than a change, because it is the more precise name for the concept and reads better in prose. What it actually costs is the alignment this decision bought: the failure codes cannot follow it — #48 fixed them, they cross the worker seam, and they are what the four reader sentences key on — so the rename ends with the glossary and the codes disagreeing, which is the state issue #47 was opened to end. The name that matches the identifiers and the codes is worth more here than the name that reads best, because the mismatch is the thing that cost four sessions their bearings.

The homonym is not the argument for reopening this. It is the known price, it is fenced by the _Avoid_ line and by the rule that `token` is always qualified, and a project with model tokens in it was never going to get a collision-free name for the other thing. What would justify reopening is the failure codes changing for their own reasons, at which point the codes and the term can move together and Protected Span becomes available at its real cost instead of at a hidden one.
